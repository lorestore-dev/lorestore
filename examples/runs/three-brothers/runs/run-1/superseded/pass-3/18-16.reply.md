# Critique

## What it is reaching for

A last page: the King's Cross conversation told so that a reader hears the tale of the
three brothers under it, with Dumbledore greeting Harry as the tale's Death greets the
youngest brother. The feeling it promises is recognition, then grief, then the choice
to go on. It is a quiet story, so it should be judged on whether its stillness carries
weight, not on event.

## Strengths

- The frame works. The opening sentence is the tale's last line, retold in the tale's
  cadence, and the close answers it ("as equals part"). The outline's first setup pays
  off cleanly.
- The confession is the right turn, and the likeness breaks where it should: "The one who
  waits at the end wants nothing" is the story's best idea, put in five words.
- "He had not lived a whole life yet" carries the choice in a single line. Keep it as it
  is.
- The thing under the bench is handled with restraint. Harry's shame at not touching it
  does more than any description would.

## Problems

1. **The explanation is summarized.** "He explained, and Harry listened", then a
   paragraph of mechanism: the story's longest stretch of reported information comes
   just where its promise is quiet conversation. The style guide forbids lecturing, and
   the paragraph lectures in the narration's voice instead of Dumbledore's. The
   reader's recognition (that Harry lives because of what Voldemort took) is the payoff
   the scene builds to, and it arrives as a summary. Direction: two or three exchanges,
   driven by Harry's questions, with the narration reduced to what Harry understands
   as it lands.
2. **The choice costs too little.** The train is offered, Harry looks at it, and he says
   no. Nothing on the page makes going on tempting, or going back hard: the Forest, the
   pain, and the people waiting get a single clause at the end. The central question
   ("will Harry choose to go back?") is answered without being asked hard. Direction:
   give the rest a concrete pull before he refuses it (what he would not have to face,
   who he might see), so the refusal costs something.
3. **The theme is stated twice.** The likeness is explained before the confession ("the
   old shape kept settling over him…") and again after it ("He was not Death. He was only
   standing where Death stands…"). The second is the stronger. Direction: cut most of
   the first. Let the greeting show the likeness, so the confession can break it without
   the narration pointing first.

The continuity and canon reviews have no open priorities that bear on these.

## Priorities
1. Dramatize the explanation as an exchange, in Dumbledore's voice, not the narrator's.
2. Give the train's rest a concrete pull before Harry refuses it.
3. Cut the first statement of the likeness, and keep the second.
