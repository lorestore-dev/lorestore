# Critique

## What it is reaching for

A last page: the King's Cross conversation told so that a reader hears the tale of the
three brothers under it, with Dumbledore greeting Harry as the tale's Death greets the
youngest brother. It moves from recognition to grief to the choice to go on, in the
hush of a fable.

## Strengths

- The frame: the tale's last line opens the story, and the close answers it.
- The explanation is now a conversation. Harry's three short questions drive it, and the
  answers land on him rather than on the reader. "Because of your mother" does what the
  old paragraph of mechanism could not.
- The choice now costs something. The train's offer is concrete (no one asking anything
  of him again, never being tired, the people who went on before), so "He had not lived
  a whole life yet" answers a real temptation.
- The likeness is stated once, after the confession, and the greeting is left to show it.
  The break lands harder for it.

## Problems

1. **One line blurs (minor).** "He has done what you could not": the reader may take
   "what you could not" as a reproach. Direction: make it plain that Harry could never
   have destroyed the piece himself without dying, or cut the sentence.
2. **One answer stays vague (minor).** "Then you might help to finish it" is the only
   case for going back, and it's abstract next to the train's concrete pull. Direction:
   one concrete thing waiting in the world, in Dumbledore's understated voice, or let
   Harry's own thought of "the people who were waiting" carry it, and cut the line.

Neither problem changes what happens. Both are line-level, for the polish.

## Priorities
1. Clarify or cut "He has done what you could not".
2. Sharpen or cut "Then you might help to finish it".
