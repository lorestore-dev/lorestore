# Outline

One chapter, one scene: the white King's Cross, between Harry walking into the Forest
and waking on its floor. Canon's events at King's Cross all still happen, in canon's
order; the story changes only their framing. The tale of the three brothers is the
frame: the narration borrows its cadence at the opening and the close, and Dumbledore
greets Harry as the tale's Death greets the third brother.

## Act structure

- **Opening (the tale's voice).** Harry wakes in a bright, empty place that slowly takes
  the shape of a railway station. The narration speaks like the last page of a fable.
- **Middle (the greeting).** Dumbledore, whole and well, meets him as an old friend.
  Their conversation runs as canon's does; the framing makes Dumbledore the figure who
  comes, at the end, for the one who did not run from him.
- **Turn (the confession).** Dumbledore admits that he himself once sought the Hallows,
  which the tale's Death never did: he is Death only as the tale's last page casts him.
- **Close (the choice).** Offered the train onward, Harry chooses to go back. The
  narration returns to the tale's cadence, and the old friend is left behind.

## Ledger

| Setup                                                                 | Payoff                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The opening sentence echoes how the tale ends: the third brother greets Death as an old friend | Harry and Dumbledore part as equals at the close, and the echo is completed |
| The station is empty; a train could take Harry "on"                   | Harry chooses to go back instead                                            |
| Dumbledore is warm, certain, almost godlike                           | His confession that he too sought the Hallows breaks the likeness           |
